Domain Expertise

Medical Devices & Pharmaceuticals

Simulation support for blood-contacting devices, drug delivery, and tissue engineering. I have published peer-reviewed research on cellular mechanics and blood damage.

How simulation helps

Medical-device development cycles are long and expensive. Early simulation work can rule out non-viable design directions before they use up hardware, animal studies, or clinical time. It can also produce evidence about physical mechanisms of the kind that supports regulatory submissions.

  • Flow analysis of blood-contacting devices: shear-stress exposure, residence time, and hemolysis prediction.
  • Modeling of microfluidic and diagnostic systems: mixing, reagent transport, and carryover.
  • Drug delivery and dissolution modeling.
  • Transport and mechanics in tissue-engineering scaffolds.

Background & selected work

My work in this area combines peer-reviewed research in cellular mechanics and blood damage with applied modeling of devices and diagnostics. Here are a few representative examples.

Tissue engineering for 3D-printed liver constructs

I contributed modeling to an implantable, 3D-printed hydrogel device with intrinsic perfusion channels (portal-venous and hepatobiliary) for liver tissue engineering. The work aims to keep engineered tissue viable by delivering blood and nutrients where they are needed.

Lieberthal, Sahakyants, Szabo-Wexler, Hancock, Spann, Oliver, et al. “Implantable 3D printed hydrogels with intrinsic channels for liver tissue engineering.” PNAS 121(47), e2403322121 (2024). Read the paper →

Real-time surrogate models for blood damage

A surrogate model is a fast approximation of a full simulation. I built deep-neural-network surrogate models that produce near-real-time visualizations of blood damage (hemolysis) in the FDA benchmark nozzle. The model handles its input data in a physics-informed way, so that it satisfies the no-slip boundary conditions automatically.

Spann, Barakat & Hancock. “Deep Neural Network Surrogate Model for Blood Damage Modeling in the FDA Hemolysis Benchmark.” COMSOL Conference. View the abstract →

Microfluidic and diagnostic systems

I use COMSOL Multiphysics® to understand and optimize fluidic diagnostic systems. The work covers concentration gradients, reagent dissolution, chemical carryover, and mixing. Rapid tests for targets such as SARS-CoV-2 and cancer biomarkers depend on this physics.

Spann. “Modeling Fluidic Diagnostic Systems.” AIChE Institute for Learning & Innovation webinar (sponsored by COMSOL). Watch the webinar →
